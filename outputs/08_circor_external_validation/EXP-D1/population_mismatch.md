# EXP-D1: adult-to-paediatric transfer, and how to describe it

Generated by `python scripts/22_cross_dataset.py`. Every number is measured.

## What was transferred to what

| | train | test |
|---|---|---|
| corpus | D1 PhysioNet 2016 | D4 CirCor 2022 |
| cohort | adult | predominantly paediatric |
| age | median 25 years (2199 of 3153 records report one) | bands only: Child 598, Infant 191, unrecorded 74, Adolescent 66, Young Adult 7, Neonate 6 |
| under 18 | 6 records (0.27%) | 861 patients (99.2% of those with a band) |

The two corpora do not even record age on the same scale -- PhysioNet in years, CirCor in bands -- which is itself part of the mismatch.

## The result

| metric | in domain (EXP-A2, 25 folds) | external (CirCor, 1 evaluation) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| sensitivity | 0.8648 | 0.3782 | -0.4866 |
| specificity | 0.8288 | 0.6734 | -0.1554 |
| balanced accuracy | 0.8468 | 0.5258 | -0.3210 |
| roc auc | 0.9212 | 0.5359 | -0.3853 |
| accuracy | 0.8362 | 0.5305 | -0.3057 |

## How this must be described

> A large drop is the EXPECTED consequence of the population mismatch recorded here and is NOT evidence that PV-MEPCG fails to generalize. Describe the result as 'cross-dataset transfer from an adult cohort to a predominantly paediatric cohort'; never as 'cross-dataset generalization' unqualified.

Three reasons the two columns above are not like for like, all of them recorded before the numbers existed:

1. **Population.** Adult degenerative disease (CAD 287, MVP 134) against a paediatric cohort in which the dominant abnormal finding is a murmur. The acoustic signature of the abnormal class is not the same object.
2. **Corpus heterogeneity inside the training set.** PhysioNet's six sub-collections behave like six different datasets: class balance runs 8.5% to 71.4% abnormal and all 20 top features by pooled Cohen's d reverse sign between sources. Any model fitted on the pooled corpus inherits that heterogeneity, so the drop has at least two causes and must not be attributed wholly to age.
3. **Different questions.** PhysioNet's label is acoustic (normal/abnormal heart sound); CirCor's `Outcome` is a clinical decision about the patient. A model can hear correctly and still disagree with a referral decision.

What the result **does** support: a screening model trained on adults should not be deployed on children without retraining, and this is the measurement that says so. What it does **not** support: any claim about PV-MEPCG's in-domain performance, which is EXP-A2's, or any claim that the method does not generalize.

## What was NOT done

No retuning of any kind. No threshold fitted to CirCor, no recalibration, no feature reselection, no partial refit. The model is byte-identical to `models_saved/binary/final/model.joblib`, fitted on 3240 PhysioNet records with hyperparameters from T07 search_space_and_best_parameters.csv.

*PV-MEPCG / PulseVision is an academic screening and decision-support prototype. It is not a diagnostic tool and must not be used to diagnose, treat, or make clinical decisions about any patient.*
