# CirCor: does the model score highest where the murmur was heard loudest?

Generated by `python scripts/21_location_analysis.py`. Every number is measured from `EXP-C1-two_class/predictions.parquet` and CirCor's `training_data.csv`.

## The question

CirCor annotates, for each murmur patient, a **Most audible location** -- the chest position at which a human judged the murmur loudest. The model never sees that field. So for every murmur-positive patient who has it, we compare the model's mean `Present` probability at that position against its mean over that same patient's other positions.

This matters because Phase 69 propagates a **patient's** label onto **every** recording from that patient. If the model were simply learning a per-patient confound -- body habitus, recording gain, ambient noise -- it would score every position of a murmur patient alike, and the difference would sit at zero.

| M5 | 169 | 0.5149 | 0.4325 | +0.0823 | 0.598 |
| M6 | 169 | 0.4276 | 0.3477 | +0.0799 | 0.639 |
| M7 | 169 | 0.4276 | 0.3477 | +0.0799 | 0.639 |

Pooled over all five models the difference is **+0.0801**, positive for 63.6% of patient-model pairs.

## Reading it

The difference is positive for every model. The model does assign more probability at the position a clinician marked loudest, without ever having been told which position that was -- so it is responding to something local to the recording, not only to a patient-level confound.

**It is not large.** A shift of roughly 0.08 in probability does not move many recordings across a 0.5 decision threshold, which is consistent with the per-location sensitivities in `T22_auscultation_location_analysis.csv` differing by less than 0.10 between the best and worst positions. The honest reading is that location carries a real but modest signal, and that a screening deployment should record multiple positions rather than rely on any one of them.
