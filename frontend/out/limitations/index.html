Limitations

What the results on this site can and cannot support. These are properties of the public corpora, not open bugs, and each one is stated in the thesis rather than worked around.
Read them before quoting any number from the model or robustness pages.

EXP-D1

The cross-dataset test is adult to paediatric

Adult-to-paediatric cross-dataset transfer. The model is fitted on PhysioNet 2016 (adult) and applied unchanged to CirCor 2022 (predominantly paediatric), scored against CirCor's clinical Outcome label.

The training corpus, D1 PhysioNet 2016, records a numeric age for 2,199 recordings: median 25.0 years, with 6 under 18 (0.3%). The test corpus, D4 CirCor 2022, has 942 patients, of whom 868 have an age band and 861 of those are paediatric (99.2%). CirCor records an age band, not an age: band, not years -- not comparable to PhysioNet's numeric age.

A large drop is the EXPECTED consequence of the population mismatch recorded here and is NOT evidence that PV-MEPCG fails to generalize. Describe the result as 'cross-dataset transfer from an adult cohort to a predominantly paediatric cohort'; never as 'cross-dataset generalization' unqualified.

PhysioNet's six sub-collections behave like six different datasets: class balance runs 8.5% to 71.4% abnormal and all 20 top features by pooled Cohen's d reverse sign between sources. Any model fitted on the pooled corpus inherits that heterogeneity, so the drop has at least two causes and must not be attributed wholly to age.

EXP-F3

Pooled PhysioNet results do not survive an unseen recording setup

Holding out one PhysioNet sub-collection at a time (6 folds), the deployed model M1 falls from a pooled AUC of 0.922 to 0.533, and from a pooled balanced accuracy of 0.843 to 0.519. AUC is rank-based, so this is not a threshold problem: the ranking is not there.

Each condition in PhysioNet 2016 was recorded on one or two setups only, so the recording source and the label never appear apart, and no method fitted to this corpus can separate them. Every pooled PhysioNet figure on this site is therefore a within-corpus cross-validation. No claim of generalization, deployment-readiness or field performance is made anywhere.

PASCAL

The PASCAL tracks are small

PASCAL set_a (4-class): 124 labelled recordings in total; the smallest class, extrahls, has 19.

  • artifact 40 recordings / 40 subjects
  • extrahls 19 recordings / 19 subjects
  • murmur 34 recordings / 34 subjects
  • normal 31 recordings / 31 subjects

PASCAL set_b (3-class): 461 labelled recordings in total; the smallest class, extrastole, has 46.

  • extrastole 46 recordings / 39 subjects
  • murmur 95 recordings / 37 subjects
  • normal 320 recordings / 124 subjects

At these sizes every model's interval overlaps every other's, so a ranking on PASCAL orders models that are not distinguishable. Below roughly a hundred training records a per-fold search fits noise. PASCAL A's artifact is a recording-quality label, so its four-class model is never a four-class cardiac classifier. PASCAL A and B are separate tasks and are never merged.

source outputs/01_dataset_audit/T02_class_distribution_and_imbalance_ratio.csv

CirCor

CirCor is the public subset only

The public CirCor DigiScope 2022 release is the Challenge's training portion: 942 patients and 3,163 recordings. The Challenge's validation and test patients were never published, so no result here is comparable to a Challenge leaderboard score, and the audited count differs from the full cohort the source documents cite.

68 patients carry an Unknown murmur label. The three-class murmur task keeps them, matching the Challenge; a two-class version on the known patients is reported beside it. CirCor labels the patient, not the recording, so every recording-level score has a patient-level counterpart under a declared collapse rule.
