Objective coverage

The six research objectives, what answers each one, and what is still outstanding.
filtered rows, rounded exactly as shown
Each of the six locked objectives of PV-MEPCG / PulseVision, quoted verbatim, against the datasets and evidence the blueprint's objective mapping requires, the modules that implement it and the files that evidence it. Every module and file named was checked to exist when the table was built.
1Normal versus abnormal classificationTo introduce a new approach for the classification of normal and abnormal heart sound recordings using a machine learning algorithm. An appropriate algorithm for auto diagnosis systems of heart diseases, capable of distinguishing most known pathological states, must be developed.PhysioNet 2016 + CirCor 2022Binary metrics, ROC/PR curves, confusion matrix, external validation.T08, T10, T16, T-S5evidence producednoneBinary results are cross-validated within PhysioNet 2016. The CirCor transfer (T16) and the leave-one-sub-collection-out test (T-S5) are the evidence on external validation, and both bound the result rather than extend it. This is a screening and decision-support prototype. The blueprint's phrase “auto diagnosis systems” is its wording, not a claim made by this implementation: nothing here diagnoses, and every result is reported as a screening signal.
2State-of-the-art reviewTo review recently published preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification techniques, as well as the state-of-the-art in phonocardiogram (PCG) signal analysis.All three dataset familiesLiterature review table, preprocessing ablation and feature-family ablation.PP-09, T17, T18, LIT-01, LIT-02evidence producednoneBoth ablations ran on PhysioNet 2016, the corpus the model is built on; comparison across noise, duration and population conditions is carried by T20-T22 under objective 4.
3Heterogeneous ensembleTo utilise ensemble machine learning algorithms that combine the predictions of several learning models into a single “ensemble” model to improve overall performance.PhysioNet 2016 + CirCor 2022Individual-vs-ensemble comparison, calibration and cross-dataset generalization.T08, T09, T19, T23, T28, T16evidence producednoneThe ensemble's members are the SVM, Random Forest and Gradient Boosting models (T06). The CirCor tracks (T13, T14) evaluate the ensemble on a second corpus under its own cross-validation.
4Short, noisy, single recordingsTo understand heart sound classification techniques that might find use in medical diagnostic systems; the objective is to accurately classify normal and abnormal heart sounds from single, short, and potentially noisy recordings.PhysioNet 2016 + PASCAL + CirCorDuration-wise, noise-wise and dataset-wise robustness tables.T20, T21, T22, T27evidence producednoneNoise and duration groups are observational; the AWGN sweep and the truncation study are their interventional counterparts on a PhysioNet subset.
5Search-driven optimizationApply a search algorithm to improve the performance of the diagnostic system in terms of accuracy, complexity, and the range of distinguishable heart sounds.PhysioNet 2016 for search; PASCAL/CirCor for confirmationBaseline-vs-optimized results, selected features, inference time and complexity trade-off.T07, T18, T19, T24, T25, T26evidence producednoneThe search ran nested inside every PhysioNet outer fold and was repeated per fold on the PASCAL and CirCor tracks, all of which are tuned runs (T11-T14).
6More classes, more samplesTo introduce a greater number of classes (types of heart sounds) to test the final system further, making it capable of handling more tests on a larger number of samples.PASCAL A/B + CirCorMulticlass confusion matrices, macro-F1, per-class recall and large-sample validation.T11, T12, T13, T14, T15evidence producednoneThe PhysioNet diagnosis track (EXP-G1, T-S1 to T-S4) was intended to carry this objective at scale; held within one recording source it does not (T30). PASCAL A has 124 records with 19 in one class, which is too few to carry this objective alone. The PhysioNet diagnosis track (3,240 records) is what answers it at scale. PASCAL's “artifact” label is a recording-quality category, not a cardiac class, so the four-class model is never described as a four-class cardiac classifier.
